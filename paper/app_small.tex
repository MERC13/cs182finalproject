\textbf{Linear attention.} The model of Section~3.2 ($d = 8$, $N = 32$, 8 heads with rank-one keys and queries, weights initialized from $\mathcal N(0, 10^{-4})$) is trained for 4,000 steps on batches of 1,024 fresh prompts with $\lambda_j \propto 1/j^2$. In the principal basis, with query covariance $\Lambda_q$ and $A = \Gamma\Lambda_q\Gamma^\top$, its loss is
\begin{equation*}
L = \tfrac1d\Big[\tfrac{N+1}{N}\,\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda A\Lambda) + \tfrac1N\,\mathrm{tr}(A\Lambda)\,\mathrm{tr}\,\Lambda - 2\,\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda\Gamma\Lambda_q) + \mathrm{tr}\,\Lambda_q\Big],
\end{equation*}
and $L^\star = 1 - \frac1d\sum_j \lambda_j\rho_j^\star$. Both are computed exactly from $\Gamma$ every 10 steps. SGD here is plain gradient descent without momentum, as in \citet{zhang2025training}; Adam has no weight decay. Muon and the knob arms update the stacked key and query matrices, with Adam at the same learning rate on the head scalars $v_h$. Each arm's learning rate is selected on seed 0 from a six-point grid by mean loss over training, and seeds 1--2 are run at that rate (Table~\ref{tab:smallhp}). For the PCR-versus-ridge comparison we clip the gain profile to $g_j = \min(\max(\rho_j/\rho_j^\star, 0), 1.2)$ and compare it with a PCR step $\mathbf 1[j \le m]$ ($m = 0,\dots,d$) and a ridge curve $\lambda_j/(\lambda_j + a)$ (200 log-spaced $a \in [10^{-3}, 10^2]$): $R_{\rm PCR} = \min_m \sum_j (g_j - \mathbf 1[j\le m])^2$ and $R_{\rm ridge} = \min_a \sum_j (g_j - \lambda_j/(\lambda_j+a))^2$. Each residual is averaged over 8 log-spaced excess-loss levels from 0.5 to 0.1, which every arm reaches (profiles interpolated linearly between logged steps), and we report $R_{\rm PCR}/R_{\rm ridge}$ per seed. The ``mean gain on directions 3--8'' in Table~\ref{tab:summary} is the mean over $j = 3,\dots,8$ of $\rho_j/\rho_j^\star$ at excess loss 0.1, from the median profile over seeds. Query shifts use the same $\Lambda^{1-2t}$ family. At excess loss 0.1, standard Muon's error relative to Adam's is 1.39 and 1.28 for the two most common-ward shifts and 0.92 and 0.95 for the two most rare-ward ones (median over seeds). At 0.05 the common-ward ratios are 1.00 and 1.02 and the rare-ward ones 0.96 and 0.97. SGD reaches excess loss 0.1 in two of three seeds and never reaches 0.05.

\textbf{Smaller GPT-2.} 4 layers, 4 heads, width 128, $d = 8$, 32 examples, batch 64, hidden initialization 0.044, the token layout of Section~3.2 and $\lambda_j \propto 1/j^2$; seeds 10 and 11 (Table~\ref{tab:smallhp}). The knob arms ($p = 1, 0.25, 0$; seed 10) all use Muon's rate 0.007, so $p=1$ is untuned and about eight times slower. Read-outs: the fraction of the weakest direction learned ($f_8$; mean of seeds 10--11) and the probe score $\bar f$ over directions 3--8 are read at error 0.03; the PCR/ridge comparison at errors 0.1 and 0.05. The query shift (seed 10 only) is read at errors 0.05, 0.03 and 0.01 of the relative error $\sum(\hat y - y)^2/\sum y^2$ on its 256 prompts. All probe scores in this model are positive at these levels, so the bounded and raw scores agree.

\textbf{Interpretation caveat.} The linear-attention profile measures the learned gain $\rho_j/\rho^\star_j$ directly, while the GPT-2 measurements infer it from predictions. The PCR-versus-ridge statistics in the two settings are therefore different measurements of the same idea and should not be compared numerically.
