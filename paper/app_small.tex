\textbf{Smaller GPT-2.} 4 layers, 4 heads, width 128, $d = 8$ and 32 examples, otherwise as in the 12-layer GPT-2 (seeds 10--11). It shows the same ordering. At error 0.03 Muon has learned 0.61 of the rarest direction, against 0.50 for SGD and 0.39 for AdamW, and the probe score over directions 3--8 rises from 0.72 at $p=1$ to 0.83 at $p=0$. \textbf{Linear attention.} With query covariance $\Lambda_q$ and $A = \Gamma\Lambda_q\Gamma^\top$ (principal basis), the loss is $L = \frac1d[\frac{N+1}{N}\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda A\Lambda) + \frac1N\mathrm{tr}(A\Lambda)\,\mathrm{tr}\,\Lambda - 2\,\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda\Gamma\Lambda_q) + \mathrm{tr}\,\Lambda_q]$. The optimal gain is $\rho_j^\star = \lambda_j N/((N+1)\lambda_j + \mathrm{tr}\,\Lambda)$, and excess loss is $(L-L^\star)/(1-L^\star)$. SGD is plain gradient descent, as in \citet{zhang2025training}. The PCR/ridge residual ratio (median over seeds, averaged over excess losses 0.5 to 0.1) is 0.50 for SGD, 0.64 for Adam, 0.58--0.80 for $p \ge 0.25$, 2.62 at $p = 0.1$, 4.86 at $p = 0$ and 3.83 for standard Muon. Out of distribution, at excess loss 0.1, Muon's error is $1.15$--$1.20\times$ Adam's on common-ward queries and $0.95$--$0.97\times$ on rare-ward ones; the rare-ward ratio falls from about 1.02 at $p = 1$ to about 0.93 at $p = 0$.
