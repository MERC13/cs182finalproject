Optimizers in in-context learning

The optimizer changes what a transformer's in-context learner learns, not only how fast. At the same in-distribution error, Muon holds a ridge-shaped predictor while SGD and Adam hold PCR-shaped ones, and that shape decides how well the model handles queries along low-variance directions.

Setup and checks

Each prompt is a sequence of points (x, wᵀx) with x in 8 dimensions and a fresh task w; the model predicts each y from the points before it, with up to 32 in context. Input covariances have eigenvalues λ_i ∝ 1, 1/i or 1/i², so some directions carry far less signal than others. We trained a small GPT-2 (4 layers, 4 heads, width 128) as in Garg et al. (2022), and one-layer linear attention, with SGD, AdamW (Adam in the linear model; SignGD there too) and Muon, each at a tuned learning rate.

Before testing anything new, the code reproduced the published results the hypotheses rest on.

Why SGD could not train the GPT-2

On the starting plateau one learning rate cannot serve every layer. With SGD at learning rate 0.1, a step would change the read-out by — of its size but the query weights by only —: any rate that moves the attention is far too large for the read-out. AdamW and Muon change every hidden layer by comparable amounts (table below). The gradient inside each matrix also points almost entirely one way, so even SGD normalised to Muon's step size per matrix stayed stuck.

SGD trains only with both changes

With separate x and y tokens the model must first copy each x into its y token, and GPT-2's small initial weights starve that circuit of gradient. Concatenated tokens remove the copy step and a larger initialisation lets the gradient through; either change alone leaves SGD stuck.

Rotating the inputs

In the GPT-2, AdamW converged at — steps with aligned inputs and — with rotated ones, inside the seed spread. The null held at batch 256 and with an identity read-in. Rotating the inputs only re-mixes the weights that feed each read-in neuron, a rotation Adam tolerates in transformers (Zhang, Maes et al. 2024). In one-layer linear attention the inputs reach the attention weights directly, and there rotation costs Adam more the larger the batch.

Transience: is Muon's in-context learning more short-lived?

With a finite pool of training tasks, a model can stop doing in-context regression and memorise the tasks instead. Both optimizers did so with 16 and 32 tasks. With 32 tasks Muon's in-context learning faded at step — and AdamW's at —, but Muon reaches every milestone sooner, so this is speed, not a different path.

Gradient noise

Small batches make Muon learn directions more one at a time, as Jacobs and Burkholz (2026) predict. The learning-order exponent β measures this: the time to learn direction j scales as λ_j−β, so β = 0 means every direction at once and larger β means a steeper staircase. In one-layer linear attention under λ ∝ 1/i², Muon's β rises from — at batch 1,024 to — at batch 16; in the GPT-2 from — at batch 256 to — at batch 16. Adam's β also rises at small batch; SGD's moves the other way.

The optimizer changes the in-context predictor

Compared at the same in-distribution error, the optimizers have not learned the same thing. SGD and Adam hold a PCR-shaped predictor: the strong directions in full and the weak ones not at all. Muon holds a more ridge-shaped one, with more directions partly learned; with an exact orthogonalisation, every direction is. Queries that load the weak directions expose the difference.

Where this leaves the project

Study one variable, the optimizer, and one question: does it change which in-context algorithm the transformer learns? Use one-layer linear attention, where losses are exact and theory exists, and the GPT-2 with concatenated tokens at initialisation 0.044, where SGD, AdamW and Muon all train. Read out each direction's learned fraction and the shifted-query error at matched in-distribution error. Hold the batch size at a large value, where the difference is clearest, and drop the rotation arm.

Caveats
  • CPU-scale runs: a 0.8M-parameter GPT-2 and a one-layer model, a few thousand steps each.
  • Few seeds: three in most cells, two in the redesigned GPT-2, one in some batch-size and transience cells.
  • One tuned learning rate per optimizer and setting, at a constant schedule. Part of the small-batch effect on Muon is parameter jitter rather than learning order.
  • In one-layer linear attention every optimizer ends at the same predictor; the differences shown are along the way, which is why they are compared at matched in-distribution error.
Definitions
Learning-order exponent β
Slope of log(step at which direction j is learned) against log(1/λ_j). Zero means every direction at once.
Muon (exact)
Muon with an exact orthogonalisation of each update (the polar factor) instead of five Newton–Schulz steps. "No momentum" variants drop Muon's momentum.
SignGD
Gradient descent on the sign of each gradient entry: Adam without its running averages.
PCR-shaped predictor
Strong directions fully learned and weak ones not at all, like principal-component regression on the top directions.
Ridge-shaped predictor
Every direction partly learned, shrinking the weak ones most, like ridge regression.
Shifted-query loss
Error when the query's variance moves onto the directions that had least variance in training, at constant total variance.
ICL score
1 when the model's error on unseen tasks matches ridge regression, 0 when it matches the estimator that only knows the training tasks.
Memorisation score m
0 at ridge-level error on the training tasks, 1 at the memorising estimator's error; it is a normalised training loss.
